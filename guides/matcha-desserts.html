Matcha Desserts — Green Tea Sweets at Home

How to use matcha in desserts: choosing good matcha, matcha ice cream, cakes, and drinks.

What matcha is

Matcha is finely ground green tea, whisked with hot water into a frothy drink, and it is also used as an ingredient in desserts. Unlike regular green tea, where the leaves are steeped and removed, matcha is the whole leaf ground to a powder, so you consume all of it, including its caffeine and antioxidants. Good matcha is vivid green, smells fresh and grassy, and tastes smooth with a gentle bitterness. It gives desserts a distinctive colour, a subtle tea flavour, and a natural green shade that needs no food colouring.

Choosing matcha for cooking

Matcha is sold in grades. Ceremonial grade is the finest, for drinking, and it is expensive. Culinary grade is made for cooking, costs less, and has a stronger, slightly more bitter flavour that stands up to sugar and cream. For desserts, culinary grade is the right choice. Buy matcha in small amounts, keep it sealed in a cool, dark place, and use it within a few months, because it loses colour and flavour when exposed to air and light. A dull, yellowish matcha is old and will give poor results, so check the colour before you buy.

Matcha ice cream

Matcha ice cream is the easiest matcha dessert and one of the best. Whisk two tablespoons of matcha with a little hot water until smooth, then stir it into a simple ice cream base of cream, milk, sugar, and a pinch of salt. Churn it in an ice cream maker, or freeze it in a container, stirring every half hour to break up ice crystals. The result is creamy, lightly bitter, and vivid green. Serve it in small scoops, because the flavour is intense, with a few berries or a plain biscuit alongside. It keeps in the freezer for about two weeks.

Matcha cakes and cookies

Matcha works well in sponge cakes, pound cakes, and shortbread, where it gives colour and a gentle tea flavour. Sift the matcha with the flour so it distributes evenly, and use about one to two tablespoons per cake, depending on how strong you want the flavour. Matcha pairs well with white chocolate, cream cheese, and red bean paste, which balance its bitterness with sweetness. Matcha shortbread is a good first bake: simple, reliable, and it keeps for a week in a tin. Avoid adding too much matcha, which makes cakes bitter and dry.

Matcha drinks and parfaits

A matcha latte is hot milk whisked with matcha and a little honey, and an iced version is refreshing in summer. Matcha parfaits layer matcha ice cream, cream, sponge cake, and red bean paste in a glass, and they look impressive with little effort. When making drinks, whisk the matcha with a little hot water first to remove lumps, then add the milk. Lumps are the most common problem with matcha drinks, and whisking the powder into a paste before adding liquid solves it.

Tips and serving

Matcha desserts are best served in small portions, because the flavour is rich and the caffeine is real: a dessert made with a tablespoon of matcha contains roughly as much caffeine as a cup of green tea. Keep matcha desserts away from strong flavours that would overwhelm the tea, and serve them with plain accompaniments. Store matcha powder sealed and cold, and buy small amounts often rather than a large tin that sits for a year. With good matcha and a light hand, the desserts are elegant, distinctive, and easy to make.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between ceremonial and culinary matcha?

Ceremonial grade is finer, smoother, and more expensive, made for drinking. Culinary grade is stronger and cheaper, made for cooking. Use culinary grade for desserts.

How much matcha should I use in baking?

About one to two tablespoons per cake or batch of cookies. Too much makes the result bitter and dry, so start with less and increase next time if you want a stronger flavour.

Does matcha contain caffeine?

Yes. Matcha contains caffeine, roughly as much as green tea per serving. Desserts made with matcha have a modest amount, but avoid large portions late at night if caffeine affects your sleep.
