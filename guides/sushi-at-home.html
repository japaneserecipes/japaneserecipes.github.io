Sushi at Home — A Beginner's Guide

Learn to make sushi rice, shape nigiri and roll maki at home with simple equipment and fresh ingredients.

Start with the rice

Good sushi starts with good rice, and that means short-grain Japanese rice, not long-grain or basmati. Rinse the rice in a bowl, changing the water several times until it runs mostly clear, then soak it for about 30 minutes. This soaking step matters because it lets each grain absorb water evenly, so the cooked rice is tender all the way through instead of firm in the centre. Cook it with the 1 to 1.2 rice-to-water ratio in the calculator on our home page, let it rest covered for ten minutes after cooking, and then season it while it is still warm. Fan the rice gently as you fold in the vinegar mixture so it cools quickly and gains a light shine.

Seasoning the rice

Sushi vinegar is a mix of rice vinegar, sugar, and salt. For each cup of cooked rice, use about one tablespoon of rice vinegar, one teaspoon of sugar, and half a teaspoon of salt, warmed just enough to dissolve the sugar and salt. Pour it over the rice and fold with a rice paddle or wooden spoon using a cutting motion, turning the bowl as you go. Do not stir in circles or press the grains, because that crushes them and makes the rice gummy. The finished rice should taste lightly tangy and slightly sweet, hold together when pressed, and still show separate grains. Keep the rice at room temperature and use it within a few hours.

Choosing fish and fillings

For home sushi, buy fish labelled sushi-grade or sashimi-grade from a fishmonger you trust, and keep it cold until the moment you use it. If you are unsure about raw fish, start with cooked fillings such as cooked prawns, crab sticks, tamagoyaki (sweet rolled omelette), or smoked salmon. Vegetables add colour and crunch: cucumber cut into thin strips, avocado, and pickled radish are all reliable. Wash your hands well, use a clean board and a sharp knife, and slice fish against the grain in smooth single strokes. A sharp knife is not a luxury here; a dull blade tears the fish and makes neat slices impossible.

Making maki rolls

Place a sheet of nori shiny-side down on a bamboo mat, spread a thin even layer of rice over it, and leave a strip of about two centimetres bare at the far edge. Lay your fillings in a line across the centre, lift the near edge of the mat, and roll firmly but gently, tucking the fillings in as you go. Seal the bare edge with a little water. Then cut the roll with a damp sharp knife, wiping the blade between cuts, into six or eight even pieces. Your first rolls will look uneven, and that is normal. The second batch is almost always better, because your hands learn the pressure quickly.

Shaping nigiri

Nigiri is a small oval of rice topped with a slice of fish. Wet your hands lightly so the rice does not stick, take about a tablespoon of rice, and shape it into a compact oval by pressing gently between your fingers and palm. Add a tiny dab of wasabi to the fish if you like it, lay the fish over the rice, and press once to join them. Do not squeeze hard. Nigiri should feel firm enough to pick up in one piece, but soft enough to come apart easily when you eat it. Serve it fish-side down on the tongue, which is how the flavour comes through best.

Serving and eating

Serve sushi soon after making it, with soy sauce in a small dish, pickled ginger, and wasabi on the side. Dip fish-side down into the soy sauce, lightly, so the rice does not soak it up and fall apart. Pickled ginger is a palate cleanser between different types, not a topping. If you made more than you can eat, cover it tightly and refrigerate it, but know that refrigerated rice firms up and the texture will not be the same; it is best eaten within a day. Making sushi at home is a skill that improves fast, and a relaxed first attempt teaches more than a perfect-looking one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a bamboo mat to make sushi?

A bamboo mat helps, but a clean tea towel or a sheet of cling film can work for your first rolls. The mat gives even pressure, so it is worth buying if you plan to make sushi more than once.

Can I use regular rice for sushi?

Short-grain rice is strongly recommended because its starch makes the grains stick together. Long-grain rice stays separate and will not hold a roll or nigiri shape.

How long does homemade sushi keep?

Eat it the same day, ideally within a few hours. If you must store it, refrigerate it covered and eat it within 24 hours. Do not freeze finished sushi.
